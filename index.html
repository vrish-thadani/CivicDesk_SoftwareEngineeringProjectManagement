Executive Summary

⚠️

The Problem

Complaints arrive through four unlinked channels (phone, web, counter, social media). The same pothole is reported eleven times and fixed zero times. Resolved complaints stay open after verbal closure. The commissioner has no reliable view, and citizens lose trust.

💡

The Core Idea & Solution

The technical problems (no linking, no dedup, verbal closure) are easy to fix. The hard problem is political: a system that exposes ward officers without their buy-in gets sabotaged. Every design choice treats the ward officer as a stakeholder with a stake in the system, not as a target of it.

Business Requirements & Scope

Scope Definition

In Scope

  • Intake from phone, web, counter and social media
  • Deduplication & routing by ward and department
  • SLA and escalation engine
  • Field closure with evidence & dashboards

Out of Scope (Phase 1)

  • Payments & contractor billing
  • Automated staff penalties
  • Predictive maintenance
  • Replacing department work-order systems

Key Business Objectives

Objective Success Measure

Stakeholder Strategy (Influence × Interest)

Why treat ward officers as stakeholders? If they are sabotaged-by-design the data is false; co-design costs weeks, sabotage costs the project.

Manage Closely (High Influence, High Interest)

  • Commissioner: Sponsor. Agree metrics up front; weekly review.
  • Ward officers: Co-design. View own data first, annotate, dispute.
  • Dept heads: Joint ownership of SLAs.

Keep Satisfied (High Influence, Low Interest)

  • Councillors, Media: Public status page; official reply templates; no surprises.

Keep Informed (Low Influence, High Interest)

  • Citizens: Ticket ID, SMS/WhatsApp status, confirm closure.
  • Field engineers: Simple mobile app, training, not blamed for upstream delays.

Monitor (Low Influence, Low Interest)

  • Operators, IT ops, auditors: Training, runbooks, audit-log access.

Deduplication Pipeline

  1. Normalise: Category, lat/long, accuracy, timestamp.
  2. Candidate search: Same category, ≤ 50m radius, ≤ 72h window.
  3. Confidence rule: Auto-merge if 1 candidate & accuracy ≤ 20m. Else queue for review.
  4. Merge: Attach as "+1", SLA clock unchanged.
Design Choice: Prefer a false split over a false merge. A false merge silently buries a real complaint.

Buffer Justification

Buffer activity F (integration, ward pilot and UAT) is on the critical path with zero float, has the widest spread (71% of path variance), and overlaps the monsoon season. Instead of padding individual tasks, one shared 3-week buffer protects the final date, preventing hidden slack and late deliveries.

Key Risks (Risk Register)

Risk Score (P×I) Mitigation Strategy
Organisational resistance (ward officers sabotage system) 4 × 5 = 20 Co-design; officers see data first; reason codes and dispute rights; normalised metrics; volunteer pilot wards.
Monsoon delays pilot 4 × 4 = 16 3-week buffer after F; indoor/UAT work first; flexible pilot wards.
Field engineers skip updates 4 × 4 = 16 Simple offline-capable app; supervisors' sign-off; photo-only closure.

Deduplication Accuracy Testing

Target: Incorrect merges < 3%

Hard Negative Edge Cases

  • Same place, same hour, different category (water leak vs pothole) → No merge.
  • Two distinct potholes 30 m apart → Operator review, not auto-merge.
  • Report across a road boundary within 50 m → Merge on distance; ticket stays with owning ward.
  • Citizen replies "different problem" → Automatic split into a new ticket.

Boundary Value Analysis (SLA)

Rule: Escalate if elapsed ≥ 80% of SLA; breach if elapsed > 100%. Granularity 1 minute.

  • Streetlight (48h) at 80%: 38h23m (no escalation) → 38h24m (escalate)
  • Streetlight (48h) at 100%: 48h00m (no breach) → 48h01m (breach)
Defects cluster at boundaries (≥ versus >, off-by-one minute). Hard-negative tests ensure false merges don't go undetected.
